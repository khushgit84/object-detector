How Object Detection & Machine Learning Work

A complete primer on deep neural networks, computer vision, and real-time detection architectures.

Concept 1
Classification vs. Detection
Image Classification looks at the whole image and outputs a single label (e.g. "Cat").

Object Detection identifies multiple objects, figures out where they are, and outputs a bounding box [x, y, w, h] for every single one with an individual confidence score.
Concept 2
The Neural Network Backbone
Deep Convolutional Networks (CNNs) pass the image through dozens of layers. Early layers find lines and edges; middle layers combine them into shapes and textures; deeper layers recognize whole object components (like car wheels, dog ears, or human faces).
Concept 3
Bounding Boxes & IoU
The model predicts four coordinates for each box. To filter out overlapping duplicate guesses, it uses NMS (Non-Maximum Suppression) and calculates IoU (Intersection over Union)—keeping only the highest-confidence bounding box.
Concept 4
Why YOLO is Revolutionizing Vision
Older algorithms (like R-CNN) ran in two separate passes: first proposing regions, then classifying them. YOLO (You Only Look Once) predicts boxes and classes in a single forward pass, reaching 60+ FPS on consumer GPUs!
Concept 5
Transfer Learning for Custom Items
Instead of training a model from scratch with millions of photos, we take the pre-trained weights (which already understand edges and lighting) and fine-tune just the final detection heads on 20–50 photos of your custom item.
Concept 6
Evaluation Metrics: mAP
Object detectors are evaluated using mAP (mean Average Precision). mAP@50 measures how often the model detects the right item with at least 50% bounding box overlap with the true location.
